I spent the last few weeks building a Python simulator of Balatro (the poker roguelike that blew up on Steam) from scratch, card rules, scoring, blinds, all of it, and training a reinforcement learning agent to play it.

Then I hooked it up to the actual game. Not the simulator, the real Balatro app, through a mod that lets outside code control it. The AI was only ever trained in my simulator, so this was the real test of whether it actually learned anything.

Over 400 real games against the live game, it wins about 75.5% of the time.

The biggest struggle I had was the AI kept breaking up a full house or flush it had just been dealt and playing something worse instead. I already had the reward punishing that behavior, so it made no sense that it kept happening.

Eventually I figured out the reward was fine the whole time. The actual issue was how I let the AI describe which cards it wanted to play. It had to pick one number out of 218 options, and each number secretly meant a different group of cards. So instead of just pointing at the cards it wanted, it basically had to memorize a phonebook. I switched it to answering a simple yes or no for each card, and also gave it a direct hint about which cards form its best hand. That combination fixed it almost overnight, full house and flush preservation jumped from under 10% correct to over 90%.

Right now it only plays Ante 1. The game gets a lot more complex after that (more bosses, more cards, more systems to model), so I kept the scope there for this first version and will push further later.
